The
affordability

how the same interest rate cycle produced four completely different housing markets and why the difference is not an accident ;]

Between january 2019 and december 2024, mortgage rates around the world followed almost the same path. But the effect on ordinary buyers in Toronto, Vancouver, London, and New York was nothing like the same! My project measures how each city responded and shows that the differences trace back to something surprisingly simple: how long people can lock in a mortgage rate

A little note M.V.

i started this project because i kept hearing the same sentence in every conversation about housing.

"it's just impossible now." my friends said it about apartments in warsaw. my cousin said it about vancouver. my economics teacher said it about london when he was talking about his students who wanted to stay after graduation and everyone agreed on the sentence, but nobody could tell me exactly how impossible, or whether it had really changed that much, or why some cities felt like they were breaking while others were only bending. so i went looking for the numbers.

what i actually wanted to know was small and specific. when the bank of canada raises the mortgage rate by one percentage point, how much worse does life get for a person trying to buy a house in vancouver, and is it worse than for someone doing the same thing in new york? that is the whole question. everything else in this project (like the dataset, the regressions, the forecasts) is built to answer some version of it.

i am eightneen and i am not an economist, but i wanted to try treating this like one would because i want to become a quantitative economist. all of the data here comes from official sources, all of the code is on github, and everything can be pulled apart and checked. if something looks wrong, it probably is, and i would rather know. Also project i started at the beggining of 2025, so some of the forecasts are already out of date, but i wanted to keep them here because they show how the model works and what it was predicting at the time.

1. Context

Four cities, one global rate cycle

Every central bank raised rates aggressively in 2022 to fight inflation. But the pain landed on households in wildly different ways

In march 2022 the Bank of Canada, Bank of England, and Federal Reserve began the sharpest coordinated tightening cycle in forty years. Within eighteen months, policy rates went from near zero to above five percent in all three countries!

On paper, buyers everywhere faced the same shock. In practice, a home in Vancouver became mathematically unaffordable, while a home in New York barely moved on the affordability scale. The gap is exactly what my project measures

"In some markets the rate hike hit borrowers immediately. In others, existing owners were essentially untouched for years. The difference is not random. It follows the length of a typical mortgage lock in each country almost exactly"
Carolyn Rogers, Bank of Canada, 2024
A 1 percentage point rise in the mortgage rate raises the typical housing burden by 9.0 points in Vancouver, 7.4 in Toronto, 7.0 in London, and 5.0 in New York. That ranking is definitely not random, as i said previously. It follows the length of a typical mortgage lock in each country almost exactly

06 · Method

How this was built.

all data is from official public sources. all analysis is reproducible from the code in the repository. any errors are mine.
Research question

To what extent do mortgage interest rates, household income, housing supply, and unemployment explain housing affordability across four major global cities (Toronto, Vancouver, London, and New York) between january 2019 and december 2024, and how accurately can these factors predict short term affordability shifts?

Data sources

House prices. CREA Composite HPI Benchmark, seasonally adjusted (Toronto and Vancouver). UK Land Registry HPI (London). FRED Case Shiller NYXRSA anchored to the 2024 metro median (New York).

Household income. Statistics Canada Table 11 10 0190 01. UK ONS regional estimates. FRED MHINY36000A052NCEN for New York state.

Mortgage rates. Statistics Canada v733833 (5 year conventional). Bank of England IUMBV34 (UK 2 year fixed 75 percent LTV). FRED MORTGAGE30US (US 30 year fixed).

Unemployment. FRED LRHUTTTTCAM156S (Canada), LRHUTTTTGBM156S (UK), NYURN (New York state).

Affordability indices

Median Multiple. Composite house price divided by median annual household income. The standard Demographia measure of long run housing cost.

MDSR (Mortgage Debt Service Ratio). Monthly amortizing mortgage payment (at prevailing rate, 25 year amortization for Canada and the UK, 30 year for the US, financed at 100 percent of price) divided by monthly household income.

Regression specification

OLS per city with the model MDSR = β₀ + β₁·rate + β₂·unemployment + β₃·log(price) + β₄·log(income) + ε.

Standard errors use the Newey West HAC estimator with a 6 month lag. Diagnostics include VIF (multicollinearity), Breusch Pagan (heteroskedasticity), and Durbin Watson (autocorrelation). The lagged rate was dropped from the final model after VIF values above 100 indicated near perfect collinearity with the contemporaneous rate.

Limitations

Household income coverage varies by country. New York state income substitutes for the discontinued NYC metro series and likely understates true unaffordability. London income relies on estimated ONS regional gross figures. Confidence intervals reflect model uncertainty but not the underlying data source uncertainty.
